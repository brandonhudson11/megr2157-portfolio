#About Me
Hello, my name is Brandon Hudson, and I am currently a junior studying Mechanical Engineering with a concentration in Motorsports at the University of North Carolina at Charlotte.

I have known for several years that I wanted to pursue engineering. My interest began with my passion for NASCAR and my goal of one day working in the sport as an engineer. Ever since I was seven years old, I have loved NASCAR and dreamed of making a career out of that passion. Throughout middle and high school, mathematics was always one of my favorite subjects, which further encouraged my interest in engineering. Although I knew I wanted to become an engineer, I initially struggled to understand what being an engineer truly meant. After several years of college and engineering courses, I have developed a much clearer understanding of the profession. I see myself as a problem solver who looks for ways to understand, fix, and improve things rather than simply replace them. I am especially interested in the mechanics of machines and the processes behind how they work.

I want to become an engineer who can apply my skills to real-world problems and develop effective solutions. What fascinates me most about engineering is the ability to combine mathematics, physics, and hands-on skills to create and improve physical systems. I want to continue developing my ability to analyze problems, use data, test ideas, and learn from the results.

My long-term goal is to become a NASCAR engineer and eventually a crew chief. As a racing engineer, I want to use problem-solving, data analysis, and testing to help improve the performance of a race car and contribute to a team. I believe one of the most important parts of engineering is learning from failure. When something does not work, I want to understand why, identify what can be improved, and apply that knowledge to the next solution. Ultimately, I am becoming an engineer who wants to solve real-world problems, continuously improve, and turn challenges into better designs.


#**What does it mean to defend an engineering decision?**

I believe defending an engineering decision means being able to talk about why you made the decision you did and provide evidence and reasoning to back up why you made that decision. Engineers should always have a reason why a decision is being made or not made. For example, if an engineer wants to design a table out of steel instead of wood, they should be able to provide reasons why they feel that steel is the better choice. Currently, I would say I do not fully understand how to defend engineering decisions, but I look forward to getting better at this aspect of engineering. I want to become more comfortable using evidence, data, and reasoning to explain and support the decisions I make.
