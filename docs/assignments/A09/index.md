# A9 – Pulley System Design

## Purpose
The goal of this assignment is to develop a pulley system that transfers power between two parallel shafts. The design must include two 3-inch pulleys positioned 4–6 inches apart and operate within a speed range of 1,000–2,000 rpm. The belt must maintain a stationary tension of 4–6 lbf and meet a minimum safety factor of 3. The project involves selecting an appropriate polyurethane belt, determining the required shaft dimensions, selecting journal bearings, and designing a supporting frame. Engineering calculations, sketches, and lessons learned will be used to explain and support the final design.

I decided to use steel for both pulleys, with a yield strength of 100,000 psi. The pulleys are designed to be 3 inches in diameter and mounted 5 inches apart from their centers. For the operating conditions, I selected a rotational speed of 1,500 rpm and a belt tension of 5 lbf. A safety factor of 3 was also chosen to provide an adequate margin against failure. These values will serve as the basis for determining the dimensions and specifications of the other components in the system.


## Belt design

The belt sizing calculations indicated that a diameter of approximately 9/32 inch would be sufficient for the design. However, since that size was not available, I selected the next larger option, a 5/16-inch polyurethane belt. Using a larger belt increases its load-carrying capacity and reduces the stress experienced during operation. This selection provides an additional safety margin, making the belt less likely to fail while ensuring the design meets the required safety factor of 3. Below is my calculations.

## Shaft Design

The shaft design process focuses on finding the smallest diameter capable of handling the torque produced by the pulley system without exceeding the allowable shear stress of the material. I selected hardened 1060 steel for both shafts and used its material properties along with the applied torque and a safety factor of 3 to determine the required dimensions. The resulting calculations gave a minimum shaft diameter of 0.128 inch. Therefore, the final shaft diameter must be at least this large to safely withstand the torsional loading during operation. ## Journal Design

After completing the shaft calculations, I moved on to choosing the journal bearings and their material. To determine a suitable option, I calculated the bearing pressure, surface speed of the shaft, and PV value. I then compared these results against the allowable operating limits listed in the material reference chart. Dry graphite was selected because its pressure, velocity, and PV ratings are sufficient for the expected loads and operating speed of the pulley system. This makes it a suitable choice for supporting the rotating shafts during operation.

## Frame Design

I designed the supporting structure as a basic rectangular frame that provides a stable base for mounting the shaft bearings. The layout keeps the shafts properly aligned while maintaining the specified 5-inch center-to-center distance between the pulleys. This design offers a straightforward way to support the rotating components and keep the system properly positioned during operation.

After reviewing the Ashby material selection chart, I chose an aluminum alloy for the frame. Aluminum provides a good combination of mechanical strength and lightweight properties, allowing the structure to support the pulley assembly without adding excessive weight. This makes it a practical material choice for maintaining structural support while keeping the overall system relatively light.

## Lessons Learned 

Throughout this project, I learned how different mechanical components must work together to create a functional and reliable power transmission system. I gained a better understanding of how to select materials, calculate shaft dimensions, determine belt requirements, and choose suitable bearings based on their operating limits. One important lesson was that design calculations do not always match the available component sizes, so selecting the next appropriate size can help provide an additional safety margin. I also learned how important material selection is when balancing strength, weight, and performance. Overall, this project helped me improve my ability to apply engineering calculations to real design decisions and showed me how important it is to consider safety, material properties, and component compatibility when developing a mechanical system.
